Public-data analysis · Cabot, Arkansas

What the crime data show before and during Flock

Cabot’s public record does not support a simple claim that license-plate cameras caused crime to fall. Serious violent crime rose, property crime was roughly flat, and vehicle theft improved. The evidence is mixed—and observational.

03 · Annual record

The year-by-year picture

Rates prevent Cabot’s population growth—about 7.4% from 2016 to 2025—from being mistaken for a change in crime. Property crime includes burglary, larceny, vehicle theft and arson.

YearPeriodPopulationViolent rateProperty rateVehicle-theft rateAll NIBRS offenses

2023 was the violent-rate peak

Cabot’s highest violent-crime rate in the 2015–2025 series occurred after the cameras were operating. The 110 offenses included 83 aggravated assaults—not a homicide surge.

Vehicle theft fell after 2023

Vehicle theft moved from 55 offenses in 2023 to 35 in 2024 and 24 in 2025. This is the clearest Flock-favorable trend, though Arkansas’s pre/post average declined even more.

Property crime rebounded

After an unusually favorable 2024, property offenses rose from 364 to 442 in 2025. Larceny increased to 360.

2015 does not change the verdict

Including 2015 in the pre-period changes exact percentages but not the overall conclusion: violent crime is higher, property crime roughly flat, and vehicle theft lower.

04 · What the system did

High activity; incomplete outcomes

Archived transparency-portal snapshots document substantial use. They do not provide a complete chain from alert to validation, stop, arrest, prosecution or recovery.

What these numbers mean

  • A plate read is not an investigation.
  • A hotlist hit is not necessarily confirmed.
  • Multiple cameras may photograph the same vehicle.
  • Use can support leads without being indispensable evidence.

What an ROI audit would need

  • Confirmed useful alerts and false-alert rate
  • Incremental vehicles recovered and dollar value
  • Case dispositions and sustained prosecutions
  • Officer time saved, annualized cost and misuse audits

These are not true cohort solve rates. A clearance recorded in one year can relate to an offense from a prior year, and exceptional clearances can occur without arrest.

About the “7 million images, 12 arrests” claim: A circulating local summary attributes 7,023,170 images and 12 arrest-and-prosecution connections to January–June 2025. The underlying city response, invoices and case list were not located for this review, so those numbers are not treated as audited facts. In any event, dividing arrests by passive observations is not a meaningful success rate.

06 · Evidence-based verdict

Useful technology is not the same as proven crime reduction

The cameras may have provided investigative leads and supported recoveries. The available citywide before/during totals cannot establish whether they caused crime to rise or fall.

Did serious crime fall?
No sustained drop. The combined violent/property rate increased 5.2%.
Did violent crime fall?
No. Its average rate increased 26.7% while Arkansas and the nation declined.
Did property crime fall?
Not relative to the pre-period or broader trend. Cabot was nearly flat while Arkansas fell 32.4%.
Did broad NIBRS offenses fall?
Yes, by 12.1%, driven by broader and more reporting-sensitive categories.
Did vehicle theft improve?
Yes. This is the strongest favorable signal, although Arkansas’s decline was larger.
Did clearances clearly improve?
Mixed. Vehicle-theft accounting improved modestly; violent weakened and property was flat.
Did Flock have investigative utility?
Probably. Usage records and city statements support leads and recoveries, but audited outcomes are absent.
Can Flock be credited causally?
No. This is an observational before/during comparison with major confounders.
Can the August 2026 suspension be evaluated?
Not yet. There is no adequate post-suspension period.

The bottom line

Cabot’s record is not a clean success story or failure story. Vehicle theft improved and broad NIBRS offenses declined. But serious violent crime rose, property crime failed to follow the statewide decline, and clearance measures were mixed. Claims stronger than that outrun the public evidence.

07 · Method & limits

What this analysis can—and cannot—answer

A causal study would require monthly go-live dates, camera locations and downtime, geocoded offenses, matched comparison cities, confirmed-hit logs, costs, case dispositions and recovery data.

Comparison rules

  • Annual offenses per 100,000 for FBI summary rates
  • Average of annual rates, not pooled counts
  • 2016–2021 pre-period; 2023–2025 during-period
  • 2022 excluded as deployment transition
  • Cabot Police Department ORI: AR0430200

Major confounders

  • COVID-era crime and reporting changes
  • Statewide and national trends
  • Staffing, enforcement and economic conditions
  • Displacement and regression after the 2023 spike
  • No comparable untreated control city

Do not add FBI summary and NIBRS values together. Their definitions and counting rules differ. Arkansas’s 2025 NIBRS table, for example, reports 29 forcible rapes and 57 burglaries, while the FBI summary series reports 27 rapes and 55 burglaries.
